Understanding Sickle Cell Disease

Understanding Sickle Cell Disease

General educational information for families and communities. This is not personal medical advice - always consult a qualified healthcare provider for your child's specific care.

What is Sickle Cell Disease?

Sickle Cell Disease is an inherited condition affecting red blood cells. Instead of the usual round, flexible shape, red blood cells can become rigid and crescent-shaped, which can block blood flow and cause pain, infections, and other complications. With appropriate, coordinated care, children living with Sickle Cell Disease can attend school, participate in normal childhood activities, and lead full lives.

Living with Sickle Cell - what families can expect

Ongoing medical care
Regular check-ups, preventive measures, and prompt attention during pain episodes or infections, guided by a qualified healthcare provider.
Nutrition
Good nutrition supports overall wellbeing. Any specific nutritional or supplement recommendations should come from a qualified professional.
School participation
With appropriate support, most children can attend school regularly. We work with schools to reduce disruption from medical appointments or hospital stays.
Emotional & social wellbeing
Living with a chronic condition can affect a child and their family emotionally. Community and psychosocial support are part of comprehensive care.

If your child is experiencing a medical emergency, seek urgent medical care immediately.
